WESTFORD — Recommendations on new school start times for the district will be presented to the School Committee before the end of the year. 


The School Start Times Subcommittee will present the School Committee with a recommendation about school start times on Dec. 16.


The subcommittee has met monthly since September of last year to discuss multiple aspects of a start time change. Topics under discussion include bus schedules, after-school scheduling, student health and how to manage a change in school start times.


Discussions about the change began last year, around the same time that the Westford Public Schools was invited to enter into an eligibility phase for potential grant funding by the Massachusetts School Building Authority. 


The conversation surrounding school start times has been suggested first in 2019 and later in 2021. In February, the subcommittee presented the impacts of early school start times on students, the benefit of later times, and suggestions for later start time schedules.


The presentation noted that schools with later start times saw increased attendance, a decrease in student-involved car accidents, an increase in student GPAs, an increase in quality of student-family interaction among other benefits.


The subcommittee has reviewed different start time options, used routing software to look at school transportation, closely explored each route, considered after-school scheduling and if start time changes would negatively affect transportation costs. 


“Can we achieve this goal without it creating super long bus rides and continue to be at the same cost level,” School Committee Vice Chair Kathryn Clear told WestfordCAT.


Changes to school start times could be seen as early as the fall of 2025, pending school committee approval after public hearings.
